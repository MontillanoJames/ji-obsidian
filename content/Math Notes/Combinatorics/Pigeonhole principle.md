> **Example 9.** To ensure that a class includes at least 2 students whose last names begin with the same letter of the (English) alphabet, the class should have at least 27 students. (Here the letters are the pigeonholes.)

A straightforward generalization of the pigeonhole principle is as follows: **If $n$ pigeonholes shelter $kn + 1$ pigeons, where $k$ is a positive integer, at least 1 pigeonhole shelters at least $k + 1$ pigeons.**

________
> [!example]
> 
> **Example 12.** A chest contains 20 shirts, of which 4 are tan, 7 are white, and 9 are blue. At the least, how many shirts must one remove (blindfolded) to get $r = 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9$ shirts of the same color?

For $r=4$, we have $r = 4 = k + 1$. So $k = 3$, and since there are 3 colors, $n = 3$. Thus, at least $kn + 1 = 10$ shirts must be removed.
